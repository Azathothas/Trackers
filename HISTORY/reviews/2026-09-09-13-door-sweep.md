## ⛔ Finding 1 -- the ceiling is a mechanism on one path and a convention on the other

`experiments/26-user-agent-block-rate.py` reaches `probe()` without going
anywhere near `sweep.plan`. It carries the ceiling because this session gave it
`--state` and `--exclude` reading the same `politeness.too_soon_after`, which
is correct -- and it is **a decision its author made, not a property of the
code**. That is the *"a control gated on one of several paths into the same
action"* row of
[`../../docs/conventions/forbidden-patterns.md`](../../docs/conventions/forbidden-patterns.md),
described there as the most recurring hole there is.

⭐ **The hole is narrower than it first reads, and the narrowing is measured
rather than assumed.** Of the four instruments that open a socket to a tracker,
only `26` draws from the population the sweep probes. The other three are
disjoint **by construction**, verified by running the production probe against
one subject of each:

```
wss://tracker.webtorrent.dev:443/announce  -> unsupported (rung none)
http://opentracker.dg2.i2p:80/a            -> unsupported (rung none)
```

`probe()` refuses both **before opening a socket**, so `25`, `35` and `36`
cannot collide with a sweep however often they run.

**So no double contact is reachable today, and the reason is a fact about the
current set of experiments rather than about the code.** Tracked as
[T-088](../../TODO/operations.md), P2, with the rejected alternative recorded:
making `probe()` itself refuse would break the loopback controls, where no
ceiling applies and no history exists.

## ⚠ Finding 2 -- two modules export a `plan()`

imports both and nothing is broken; it is recorded because the next reader
greping `plan(` gets both and has to work out which is which -- as this pass
did.
